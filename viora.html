The problem

Dehydration is invisible until it is already a problem.

Mild dehydration slows thinking, causes headaches and raises the risk of heat illness, yet most people only notice it when they are already short of water. The tools that measure it properly sit in labs and clinics. The people most exposed to heat rarely have access to them.

Thirst arrives late

The body signals thirst after water loss has begun. Children absorbed in play and older adults, whose sense of thirst weakens with age, often miss it entirely.

Testing needs a lab

Blood tests and urine analysers are accurate but need equipment, trained staff or privacy. None of them fit a school field, a building site or a village street.

Heat is rising

Heatwaves are becoming longer and more frequent. Every extra hot day puts students, outdoor workers and elderly people at greater risk, especially where cooling is scarce.

The idea

A strip that changes colour with your hydration.

Purple cabbage contains anthocyanins, natural dyes that change colour with pH. When you are short of water you make less saliva, it carries less bicarbonate and becomes more acidic. The strip turns pinker. Drag the slider to see it.

How it works

From a vegetable to an answer in four steps.

  1. Cabbage

    Boil purple cabbage, keep the dark purple water. That extract is the indicator.

  2. Strip

    Soak filter paper squares, dry them and stick two on a strip. One stays dry as a control.

  3. Photo

    Touch the test pad to your tongue, lay the strip on the printed card and take a photo.

  4. Answer

    The phone corrects the lighting against the card, reads the colour and tells you how hydrated you are.

Who it helps

Built for the people heat reaches first.

Viora is cheap enough to hand out by the hundred and simple enough to use without training. These are the groups we designed it around.

Schools in hot regions

A class-wide check before sports day or an outdoor assembly. Teachers see who needs water before anyone faints in the sun.

Morning check, under a minute per student

Outdoor workers

Construction crews and delivery riders work through the hottest hours. A strip in the pocket turns “drink more water” into a number they can act on.

Midday check on the job

Older adults and carers

Thirst fades with age, and dehydration in older people is easy to mistake for confusion or tiredness. Carers get an objective daily check.

Daily baseline, alerts when it drops

Sports teams

Players test before and after training and the coach sees the whole squad on one dashboard, with anyone who is dry highlighted at the top.

Live team dashboard, built in

Community health workers

In places without a lab, a health worker with a phone and a stack of strips can screen a whole street during a heatwave and prioritise who to help first.

Works offline, no lab needed

Our early evidence

The colour moved with hydration.

10 to 12 hwithout water
5 to 15 minafter drinking
1 to 3 hwithout water

In our first trial, saliva collected after ten to twelve hours without water turned the indicator the pinkest of all three samples, exactly as the chemistry predicts. We also showed that salt concentration alone did not change the colour, so the strip is reading pH rather than saltiness.

The trial was small: one person, two samples per condition, and the sample taken just after drinking did not sit neatly between the other two. We say so openly. That is why the app includes a study mode, a reference card and a control pad, so the next round of testing can prove, or disprove, that the strip works.

Impact plan

From one experiment to thousands of strips.

Phase 1, now

Validate

  • Recruit 30 volunteers at school
  • Record every reading against the urine colour chart
  • Train and test the model on real data

Phase 2, next term

Pilot

  • Run with school sports teams through the hot months
  • Coaches use the live squad dashboard
  • Measure how often players drink after a low reading

Phase 3, year one

Share

  • Publish the card, strip recipe and app openly
  • Strip-making workshops for other schools
  • The app is already available in Hindi and Marathi

Phase 4, beyond

Scale

  • Partner with NGOs running heat action plans
  • Kits for care homes and construction sites
  • Train community health workers to screen during heatwaves
