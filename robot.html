Our robot · HYPERDRIVE 2026

Aryan is OVERDRIVE's robot for HYPERDRIVE 2026. It is built from the NRL kit, with a parallelogram arm, a two-finger grabber and a cargo tray on a four-wheel-drive chassis. We designed it around three jobs: pick up a CORE reliably, score it on a High Pole, and finish on the Space Station. It stays under the 8 kg limit and inside the 18 × 18 in. footprint.

Subsystem

Four traction wheels, each on its own motor, so the robot drives straight and turns on the spot. It stays inside the 18 × 18 in. footprint for the whole match.

  • 4 motors
  • 4 × 110 mm wheels

Subsystem

A parallelogram arm lifted by one servo. The linkage keeps the grabber level while it rises, so a CORE stays flat on the way up to the High Pole.

  • 1 servo
  • ~256 mm max height, arm fully up (230 mm in the CAD pose)
  • full lift in ~1.5 s
  • parallelogram linkage

~ Approximate: from the CAD or our best estimate, not yet measured on the real robot.

Subsystem

A two-finger gripper on one servo. It closes on a CORE, holds it while the arm moves, and opens to release it over the pole.

  • 1 servo
  • opens to ~135 mm
  • rubber grip pads

~ Approximate: from the CAD or our best estimate, not yet measured on the real robot.

Subsystem

An open tray mounted on top of the chassis at the back, just right of the centre line. It carries the second CORE and our Payload, on a chute tilted 30° down to the back.

  • bay 114 × 138 × 81 mm
  • funnel centres the payload
  • printed PETG, ~70 g (CAD estimate: ~110 g)
  • gate parts ~19 g
  • payload ~180 g (CAD estimate: ~100 g)

The payload is a 64 mm cube with chamfered edges and "009" on its faces.

~ Approximate: from the CAD or our best estimate, not yet measured on the real robot.

Electronics layout

Battery → Main switch → HEXA Command Hub → 4 drive motors, arm servo, grabber servo, door servo

IMU, encoders, ToF sensor → HEXA Command Hub inputs

The battery feeds the HEXA Command Hub through a main switch. The hub drives the four wheel motors, the arm servo, the grabber servo and the cargo door's micro servo. The IMU and encoders report back to the hub.

Free body diagram 2 of 4

To speed Aryan up, the tyres push back on the floor and the floor pushes the robot forwards. That push has three ceilings: tyre grip (μ W), motor torque (4 τ / r), and the acceleration at which the front tyres lift off (g b / h). The lowest one wins.

Free body diagram 3 of 4

The arm is a parallelogram, so the gripper stays level and slides round on the ends of the links. Its weight acts as if it hung from the link ends, wherever its own centre of mass is. The servo holds (gripper + CORE) × g × L cos θ, plus the links' own weight at of their length.

Free body diagram 4 of 4

On a slope the weight splits in two: one part along the slope, trying to roll the robot back down, and one part into the surface. To stand still the tyres need that much friction, so μ has to be at least tan θ. The robot tips over backwards once its weight line passes behind the rear tyres: tan θ = b / h.

Engineering iteration: the grabber

  1. ProblemOur first gripper dropped COREs when the arm lifted, because the plastic fingers slipped.
  2. SolutionWe added rubber pads and moved the servo closer to the fingers for a firmer grip.*
  3. ResultGrip held in 18 of 20 test lifts*, up from 11 of 20*.

Key learning. Testing showed that small mechanical adjustments can change the robot's overall performance. Iterating on the design helped us find weaknesses and fix them based on what we saw.

* Placeholder value, not yet measured.
