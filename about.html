Match plan

  1. Pre-match30-second huddle. Read the star map on the Trifron and say the order out loud. Each robot owns one star pole and one apex pole. No contact with the opponent.
  2. 0:00–1:00Build the count. Grab our 3 spike cores, then fetch from the source zone, 2 cores per trip, to our high poles.
  3. 1:00–2:00Star map. Once Starburst is on, fetch golden cores from our vault and build in the exact map order.
  4. 2:00–2:30Level up. At 1:55 the Strategist calls "Station." Climb, then Dock (8) or Balance (15). Apex cores (+9 each) only once we have scored them on the real field.

Add 2 apex cores (18) and swap Dock for Balance = 80, plus the payload if it's built

Score targets are per robot and are our estimates until we test them. In our simulator the Apex Pole is at the very edge of a kit robot's reach from the deck, so apex cores stay in the stretch plan. Checkpoints: at 0:50, if the squad isn't at about 5 cores we stay on high poles; at 1:30, if a star map isn't within reach we switch back to high poles.

Who does what

Driver

Drive, score and climb. Use the one-button macros and listen for the Strategist's calls.

Human Player

Keep the source zone stocked (max 6 cores there at once). Stay behind the wall line when our robot is in the source zone, and never touch our robot or golden cores.

Strategist

Read the map, run the checkpoints, watch the clock, and watch for fouls.

Fouls hurt

A Major Foul hands the other squad 15 points, more than our Dock. So: no contact inside an opponent's footprint, never dock on their station, never de-score their cores, enter their source zone or launch a core, and never pin anyone for more than 3 seconds.

Software · RL policy

Our autonomous routine drives by dead reckoning, so every flaw in a real robot ends up as position error. A CORE only scores if it is released within about 0.4 in of the pole, and 1° of heading error over 100 in puts the robot 1.75 in to the side. So we trained two small neural networks with evolution strategies, on millions of simulated robots with random defects, then tested them in the full physics simulator.

84%less sideways drift after a turn (1.79 to 0.28 in)
27%less distance error out of a turn (0.63 to 0.46 in)
9.1average auto points a match, up from 8.2
19/20robots docked at the end of auto, up from 17

The drive policy has 10 inputs, 16 hidden units and 2 outputs: 210 weights, 840 bytes of flash, under 50 µs per run on the robot's ESP32-S3.

Software · what we learned

Our first real test of v2 did worse than the simple time model we were trying to beat. Finding out why taught us more than any training run.

  1. The problemAfter a 90° turn the IMU read about -7 in/s while standing still
  2. Why it hurtv2 trusted it, so it overshot forward and stopped short in reverse
  3. The fixMeasure the offset on tick 2, when the robot is still, and subtract it
  4. RobustRetrain with random offsets of ±15 in/s

A policy trained on a perfect sensor learns to rely on it. Train with the noise the real robot has.

Autonomous challenge

Three objectives: collect a CORE, score it on the High Pole, and finish balanced on the Space Station ramp. Our priority is consistency: one route that completes all three tasks reliably before adding anything else.

  1. 1Drive between the High Poles
  2. 2Turn and grab the spike CORE
  3. 3Reverse, turn and score on the High Pole
  4. 4Drive to the station set point
  5. 5Climb the ramp and balance

Full route: 23.5 s*, 7 of 10 runs*. In simulation the robot reached the High Pole but did not always stop in range, so we now align the CORE, not the robot body, using the arm's reach. Station balance is the priority: no optional action is attempted if it risks the balance.

Changemakers challenge

During one of our long lab sessions a team member fainted from a very common problem: dehydration. Viora is a saliva-based colour strip made with anthocyanins from red cabbage. They change colour with pH, so the strip explores whether changes in saliva can hint at dehydration. You compare the colour with a reference chart.

Salt test
0% to 3% salt at pH 7: no colour change
Saliva test
A clear colour difference between hydrated and dehydrated samples

For students, engineers, athletes, outdoor workers and people in hot regions. Saliva is affected by food, drink, oral hygiene, time of day, exercise and illness, so Viora can indicate a possible change but cannot confirm dehydration on its own.
